PDF Compression Guide: Reduce File Size Before Sharing

Published: December 20, 2024 | By onPDF Team

Large PDF files can be difficult to share via email, messaging apps, or upload forms. Compressing your PDF reduces its size, making it faster to send and easier to store. In this guide, we'll explore several methods to shrink your PDF without noticeably degrading quality.

Why Compress a PDF?

  • Email providers often limit attachments to 25MB or less.
  • Uploading large files to cloud services takes longer.
  • Compressed PDFs load faster in browsers and mobile devices.
  • Saves storage space on your device or server.

Method 1: Resize Images Before Conversion

The most significant factor in PDF size is the resolution of the images you include. If you're converting JPGs to PDF, reducing image dimensions or compressing them beforehand can dramatically shrink the resulting PDF. Use an image editor to resize images to a maximum width of 1200–1500 pixels for documents, or lower for web viewing.

Method 2: Use JPEG Compression

When creating a PDF from JPG files, ensure the images are saved with moderate JPEG compression (quality 70–80%). Our tool uses the original images; if they are already heavily compressed, the PDF will be smaller. Avoid using PNG images if file size is a concern, as PNGs are lossless and often larger.

Method 3: Optimize PDF with Online Tools

After generating your PDF, you can use third-party PDF compression tools (like Smallpdf, iLovePDF, etc.) to further reduce size. These tools often remove redundant metadata and recompress images.

Method 4: Choose the Right Page Size

Larger page sizes (like A3) require more pixels to fill, potentially increasing file size if images are scaled up. Use the smallest page size that still displays your content clearly. A5 is half the area of A4 and can save space when appropriate.

Method 5: Limit Number of Pages

Each page adds to the overall file size. If your PDF contains many pages, consider whether all are necessary. Remove blank or duplicate pages before converting.

How onPDF Helps

Our converter automatically centers and scales images to fit the chosen page size without unnecessarily enlarging them. By default, it doesn't add extra data or metadata, keeping the PDF as lean as possible.

For best results, start with reasonably sized images (not multi-megabyte photos) and select an appropriate page size. That way, your PDF will be small enough to share anywhere.
